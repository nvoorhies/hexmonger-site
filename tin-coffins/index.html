Wake protocol

Wake protocol — Line Talon, iteration 22.

You are a combat clone. Your body was printed this shift; your engram — reflexes, doctrine, the feel of the stick — was restored from the Talon template Command keeps on file.

What did not come back: your memories. Each iteration is scrubbed clean before it flies.

So the number on your hull is not a callsign. It is a version. You are Talon-22. There were 21 before you. They flew this same city. Most did not come home.

Deploy when ready, pilot.

The war

Infantry in someone else’s war

You are not a god in a giant robot. You are a soldier in an armored suit, four meters tall and one bad decision from dead. Cover, sightlines and reload windows decide fights. Peek, fire, displace.

Squads

They fight as a squad. So do you.

A commander and its pilots

  • Every enemy squad has a commander handing out roles — anchor, flank, suppress, overwatch, retreat — to pilots who decide for themselves when to peek, push, reload and fall back.
  • They only shoot at you when they can see you. Pop smoke and they lose you; break line of sight and they search where you were.
  • Rookies lose their nerve under fire and drop off comms. Veterans hold the order.

Three wingmates

  • Ping a target or a position, or open the comms radial: tag, flank left or right, overwatch, follow, disengage.
  • They answer on the radio and move, and they check their line of fire before they shoot. Friendly fire is real — check yours.
  • Lose an arm or your head and keep fighting. Lose your torso or your legs and you go down.

The campaign

One life. The war keeps the score.

A run is four sorties on one life, with no respawns. Purge, sabotage, extraction, seize and defense missions, and multi-stage operations that chain them — each fought in its own procedurally generated city, by day, at dusk, at night or under a toxic haze.

The front line moves with every sortie, across three acts that each end in a finale operation. Let it collapse and you hold the line. The salvage you bank buys weapons that outlast you; the casualty board keeps the names of the ones who didn’t.

After a sortie, open the Tactical Replay and scrub through the fight: what each enemy pilot knew about you against where you really were, and what their commander told them to do about it.

Echoes

Listen to yourself

The iterations before you learned to leave things in the world, off the route, where the scrub can’t reach: dead drops where earlier clones fell, and transmissions on a channel Command doesn’t log. The messages in your journal are from you. Follow them if you want to know why you keep waking up.

Eleven citations in all, each a Steam achievement. Every one reads as a commendation — until you know what the war is.
